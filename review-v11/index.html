CoverSure/Policy Portfolio/Version 11 review

It is not a word problem. It is a weighting problem.

A six-lens review of V11, with every proposed cut adversarially challenged before it was allowed to stand. The surface is carrying paraphrase while the See more panels carry the judgement. The fix is to swap them.

12 October 2026·Review of V11·35 agents, 30 of them challenging the other five

00 · Correction first

A number I reported was wrong.

Corrected 12 Oct 2026

V11 was published with the claim that its surface was 2,120 words against V10’s 2,347 — a 227-word reduction. That was wrong. The measuring script stripped <svg> before counting, hiding ~470 words of diagram labels, and the engine simulator’s copy lives in the script block where it was never seen at all.

Counted like for like, V11 is roughly 40 words heavier than V10, not 227 lighter. There was no reduction.

build-copy.py had the same blind spots plus a third: no handler for the decision blocks, so all five were missing from COPY-V11.md — a file whose own header claims it cannot drift from what is published. It had drifted by 676 words. Both are fixed.

The lesson for any future measurement here: the surface is not just <p> tags. It is SVG text, table cells, definition lists, and copy that lives in JavaScript. Count all of it, or do not quote a number.

01 · The diagnosis

Sixty-four things are bold. So nothing is.

Bold runs64across 11 sections. §05 alone has 14.
Scan layer590words of heading, bold and label
Time to skim it2:34at 230 wpm
What a founder gives0:60~230 words

The layer that exists to be skimmed is 2.6× over the budget of the person it was built for. Headings are not the problem — all eleven total only 94 words. Bold text totals 468. One section carries a single 26-word sentence entirely in bold.

The second finding matters more. The surface carries paraphrase; the See more panels carry the judgement. The sharpest line in the whole case study — “not where to place a CTA” — sits inside a dialog that a scanning founder never opens.

So the answer is not subtraction

Every large surface cut proposed by the reviewers was tested and rejected — the rule-set rows, the shipped card copy and the simulator’s reasoning are each the only instance of their content. What there is instead is ~250 words of restatement, and ~200 words of the best thinking sitting one click out of reach.

Substitute at roughly constant length. Then cut hard in See more, where it costs nothing.

02 · The change that does the most

One bold claim per section.

Sixty-four runs become eleven. No content leaves the page — it simply stops competing for the eye. This single change takes the scan layer from 590 words to 266, which is 69 seconds: inside the budget, with three seconds to spare.

04 · Show, don’t tell

Four places where copy should become a component.

  1. §05 — the ten-row table becomes a live deck

    Reuse the simulator’s phone frame. Three slots hold still while the engine band cycles the seven shipped cards. The argument of the section is the motion, so the table that states it can go. The card copy stays — as UI inside a phone it reads as shipped evidence rather than as a list.

    −143 surface words

  2. §03 — five toggle chips for the five signals

    The reader flips signals on and off; matching rules light up and the winning one rises to the top. Turn everything off and the screen says nothing relevant → nothing promoted. They produce the engine’s central constraint with their own hands, which no sentence can do.

    −48 surface words

  3. §10 — an empty dashboard

    Seven metric tiles with dashes where numbers will go, and a chip reading awaiting first cohort. One tile is struck through: recommendation click-through — “not the goal”. The honesty about having no data stops being an apology and becomes an instrument. Nothing counts up; motion here would imply data that does not exist.

    the only section with no visual at all

  4. §11 — colour the pipeline by voice

    Redraw the engine diagram with ownership as colour: solid accent for I built, outlined for I connected, grey for Product owns. Three comma-chains of 143 words become a picture that answers “what did you own” by showing where in the system it sits.

    −45 surface words

05 · Accuracy, not style

Three claims that have to change.

§The claimProblem
01“the one screen almost everyone visits”A frequency claim with no data behind it. Replace with the structural version — nothing else in CoverSure could reach it.
05“Users stop scanning it and start ignoring it”Asserts measured user behaviour. Reword to which is how a recommendation surface turns into banner space.
10“More exploration… is the intended effect, not a measured one”Delete, do not merely unbold. The hedge does not retract the outcome it plants, and the project log already names this exact shape among claims that must not come back.

§02 also says “Portfolio is where they land” and “Portfolio is where those visits land” — the same sentence twice, in the same section.

06 · Defended

What reviewers wanted to cut, and why it stays.

These survived the challenge

§05’s shipped card copy. The only shipped-product evidence on the page, and the only gloss for HospiCash and Super Top-Up. It moves into the phone frame; it does not leave.

§03’s eight rule rows. The only evidence for “every pitch card leads with a price”, and the only place the non-pitch Add your policy card appears — which is what proves the strategy is not selling-first.

The simulator’s reasoning lines. Diagnosed correctly as repetitive, but deleting them removes the best copy on the page. They are the engine explaining itself.

§07’s “rather than in the pitch band”. The only named rejected placement in the entire document.

Thirty verification agents challenged every proposed cut, and the majority of them were rejected on the grounds that they would have cost design evidence or product context. That rejection list is as much the output of this review as the recommendations are.

08 · Order of work

What to do first.

  1. The bold pass and the eleven headings. Mechanical, one sitting, and it buys the entire 55% scan-layer reduction on its own.
  2. The three accuracy fixes. Small, and they close the only places a sharp founder could push back.
  3. §10’s empty dashboard. The only section with no visual, and the change that turns an apology into a demonstration of measurement literacy.
  4. §05’s deck and §03’s signal chips. The two biggest builds, best done one at a time.
