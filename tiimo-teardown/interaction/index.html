Design the moment the plan breaks.

The teardown's central claim is that a planner for executive dysfunction is judged when the user fails, not when they plan. This takes that claim literally: ten findings, each answered with an interaction pattern, and a working prototype you can break on purpose.

Read this first

This is not a redesign of Tiimo's screens. The teardown never saw them, so there is nothing honest to redraw. The prototype uses a neutral visual language of its own, because the teardown warns against borrowing Tiimo's pastel look as a shortcut to trust.

Each pattern names the finding it answers and carries that finding's evidence tag. Where the finding is reported or unverified, the pattern is a hypothesis to test against the real app, not a claim about what the app does today. Not affiliated with Tiimo AS.

    03 — Motion

    One personality: steady. Nothing bounces.

    The audience meets this app at its most anxious moments: a missed activity, a day coming apart. Motion that celebrates or startles there is noise. So the system has one easing for almost everything, three durations, no overshoot anywhere, and linear motion in exactly one place, the time bar, because time is linear.

    TokenValueUsed for
    Standard easingcubic-bezier(.2, 0, 0, 1)Most motion: reflow, state changes, the chevron
    Enter easingcubic-bezier(.23, 1, .32, 1)Things arriving: cards, toasts, the check stroke
    Sheet easingcubic-bezier(.32, .72, 0, 1)Bottom sheets, matching the iOS drawer curve
    Quick · 120 mspress, togglesFeedback the finger is waiting for
    Standard · 240 msenter, toasts, checkSomething new needs to be noticed
    Slow · 360 msreflow, sheetsSomething moved and the eye must follow it
    Lineartime bar onlyConstant motion that represents constant time

    Rules

    • At most one thing moves on the Now view at a time. Ambient motion competes with the one thing the user is trying to start, and task initiation is the difficulty the Now view exists for.
    • Exits are faster than entrances: 160 to 220 ms out, 240 to 360 ms in. The system answers fast, and the user gets time to read what arrived.
    • Frequent actions don't animate. The options sheet slides because it is occasional. Scrolling the day, switching days and keyboard shortcuts don't.
    • Same interaction, same motion, every time. For this audience predictability is an accessibility property, not a style preference.

    04 — Haptics

    Haptics mark transitions, never failure.

    The product positions its focus timer to "manage transitions without losing momentum" observed. A haptic is the one cue that works with the phone in a pocket and eyes elsewhere, which makes it valuable and makes misuse expensive: a buzz on a missed activity is a notice of failure delivered to the body.

    MomentPatterniOSWhy
    5 min before an endTwo soft taps, 90 ms apartUIImpactFeedbackGenerator(style: .soft), intensity 0.6, twiceThe transition warning. Felt, not read
    Next activity startsOne medium tapUIImpactFeedbackGenerator(style: .medium)Marks the boundary
    DoneSuccessUINotificationFeedbackGenerator .successAcknowledges the user's own action, once, small
    Item lands after a move or reflowOne light tapUIImpactFeedbackGenerator(style: .light)Confirms where it went without looking
    A choice is made (keep going, let go, a version)Selection tickUISelectionFeedbackGeneratorRegisters the decision, rewards nothing
    Missed, overrun, sync failure, AI timeoutNone—Not the user's action, or not an error. A buzz would read as blame
    Scrolling, opening a sheetNone—Too frequent. Haptics that are everywhere stop meaning anything

    Rules

    • Outside the app, iOS offers no custom patterns. A notification plays the system haptic. So the five-minute cue travels as a Time Sensitive notification, which can break through a Focus if the user allows it, and on Apple Watch as a WKHapticType.notification tap. The in-app pattern is a bonus, not the mechanism.
    • Haptic, sound and visual cues are separate switches. Sensory sensitivity varies widely in this audience, so one person's helpful tap is another's alarm.
    • At most one haptic a second, except selection ticks during a drag. Respect the system haptics setting.
    • Android: map to HapticFeedbackConstants (CONFIRM, CLOCK_TICK) or VibrationEffect compositions, under the same restraint. A notification channel can carry a custom vibration pattern for the cue.
    05 — Errors and loading

    Every error answers three questions: what happened, is my plan safe, what now.

    The reported failures cluster around trust: sync that fails, moves that lag or crash, AI that misreads reported. For this audience an error message has a second job beyond explaining: confirming that the plan they rely on is intact.

    StateWhat the user seesWhat the system doesNever
    Missed activity"Stretch didn't happen. That's fine." Fit it in at the next gap, or let it go todayFinds the first gap that fits; keeps undo for 5 sRed, "overdue", a count of misses, a guilt notification
    Several missed at onceOne grouped question: "3 things didn't happen"Offers to fit them all into the afternoon in one moveThree separate prompts in a row
    Overrun"12 min over. No problem." Two choices, each with its consequenceMoves flexible items only, and around fixed onesShifting the day without asking; silently eating a break
    OfflineOne chip: "Offline · saved on this phone"Queues writes and marks each waiting itemA blocking spinner; "Something went wrong"
    Calendar conflictBoth versions side by side, labelled by sourceKeeps both until the user picksLast-write-wins, silently
    Sync failing for a day or moreA banner with the last successful sync timeRetries with backoff; offers export of todayHiding it until data is lost
    AI misreads or times out"Your day hasn't changed." Retry, or write it yourselfCommits nothing before the user acceptsAdding AI output straight into the plan
    Notifications deniedThe Now view and widgets still carry every cue; one explanation in SettingsFalls back to on-screen and widget cuesRe-asking for permission on the timeline
    PaywallAppears only when a Pro feature is tappedRemembers a dismissalInterrupting the Now view (teardown §08)
    CancellingTwo taps from Settings; the end date is shownConfirms by emailA retention maze; guilt copy (teardown §08: "indefensible")

    Loading

    • The timeline never shows a spinner. The day lives on the phone first. The network is for syncing, not for seeing your own plan.
    • Moves and edits apply instantly and sync behind. A failed sync shows on the item, not in a modal.
    • Only work the user is waiting for gets a loading state, such as an AI draft or a first calendar import. Progress shows as real steps arriving, never as an invented percentage.
    • Anything that can take more than about 8 seconds gets an exit and a reassurance: "Your day hasn't changed." The prototype stalls after 3 seconds so you don't have to wait.
    • Loading, result and error are one surface changing state, not three screens replacing each other.
    06 — Edge cases

    Irregular days aren't the edge case here. They're why the app exists.

    A happy-path planner assumes the day goes roughly to plan. This audience installs the app because it doesn't. These are the cases that break a happy-path design.

    Accessible by construction, then tested on a device.

    08 — Validation

    What would prove these patterns wrong.

    Each pattern is a hypothesis until tested. The first three checks need nothing more than the hands-on session the teardown already asks for.

    1. Field-guide step 4: miss an activity. If the app already reflows humanely, pattern 3 is a refinement, not a fix, and should be presented that way.
    2. Step 9: leave it for a week. If re-entry already avoids a backlog, pattern 8 confirms existing work rather than correcting it.
    3. Step 7: the accessibility pass. Greyscale screenshots of every state settle the colour question in an afternoon.
    4. The teardown's diary study (five ADHD users, six weeks): does the five-minute cue get switched off? Switched off by week two would mean it is noise, not help.
    5. Instrument the tray: fit in, let go, or ignored. A growing "ignored" share is the signal that the tray itself has become a guilt list, which is the failure this whole page is designed against.
