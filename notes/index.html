02 The problems

Source · log §5 Q6 — brought by the product head

These are the actual problems that started the work, and they are more specific than "five capabilities needed to be discoverable":

  • Users couldn't navigate to policy details properly.
  • Promotional banners were confusing them.
  • Nobody was adding family members — because no family overview existed.
Why this matters for the writing

Banner confusion was raised by the product head as a problem to solve — it was not a business preference you argued down. Log correction #4 explicitly removed an adversarial framing for exactly this reason. Any headline implying you fought for this is inaccurate.

03 The condition system

Source · index.html · log §5 Q3

Seven capabilities, each attached to a condition rather than a permanent slot. The screen composes itself from whichever conditions are true, and never offers more than two actions at once.

CapabilitySurfaces whenWhere
Add PolicyAlways — the screen's primary jobHeader
Family ManagementA family member or their policy is missingFamily overview
CoverRiskNo risk report exists yetBelow the policies
Buy PolicyA report exists and a protection gap is openBelow the policies
KYPAlways — on every policy ownedPolicy card
Advisor PortfolioThe CoverRisk report has just been generatedAfter the report
Share PolicyPolicy is Life or GroupPolicy details

The governing rules: the pitch always sits below the policies, and the ceiling is two actions at a time. Adapt closes the loop — the user acting produces new information, which is why the next recommendation improves.

06 The brand principle

Source · log §5 Q5

CoverSure's existing principle

Never sell a policy without proper information and the right coverage amount, and don't push a low-premium policy just because it converts — a low cover amount fails people at the moment it matters.

Your work applied this existing company principle to the screen where the temptation to break it was strongest. That's a stronger and more honest claim than presenting the ethics as personally invented — and it's currently missing from the scan version.

Evidential status

"Users came to manage, not to be sold to" is informed judgement, not a formal study. Your log says it must be framed as a stated design hypothesis. Anything that states it as a research finding overstates what you have.

09 Your design decisions

Source · log §2, §6

DecisionYour rationale
Identity from the product surface — policy navy, electric blue, cool paperThe cream / serif-display / terracotta alternative is the most common look in AI-generated design work and reads as templated.
Avenir Next Condensed + Charter + SF Mono, all system-availableThe artifact CSP blocks font CDNs; a linked webfont would silently fall back and wreck the type.
Points labelled by skill, not numbered 01–05Five parallel capabilities are not a sequence. Numbering would be decoration pretending to be information.
Adapt drawn on the return edge, not as a terminal boxAdapt is what closes the loop. Drawing it as a final stage would misrepresent the mechanism the diagram exists to explain.
Screens in fixed-height scrollable frames, not croppedOne export is 6,000 px tall. Cropping picks the reader's conclusion for them.
Before/after pair left as a CSS reconstructionThe "before" is a hypothetical the product never shipped. Presenting a mockup as an export would be a false claim.
No metric or performance chartsNo mature usage data. Diagrams of logic are verifiable; a fabricated adoption chart is the fastest way to lose a senior reviewer.
Shipped share copy quoted alongside the refined lineThe refined line hasn't shipped. Implying it had was the one claim a reviewer could disprove by opening the app.
The inherited PRO banner named explicitlyA reader comparing copy to screenshot would spot it. Naming what was outside the remit is stronger than silence.
Tier bars labelled "relative priority — not measured data"They are the only quasi-quantitative element and would otherwise read as measurement.
Generic policy names, not real insurersThe names in the designs are placeholders, so real ones would be fabricated.

13 Things that don't add up

Found while cross-checking the log against the repo

1 · "Sixteen real screens" — wrong unit

Log §9 lists 6 exports in the first pass and 5 in the second — 11 files, which matches assets/src/ and assets/web/ exactly, and matches the 11 unique data URIs in the page.

"Sixteen" counts placements, not exports — several screens appear in more than one section (Screens 01 and 07 in the full study are the same file). README.md:47 says "sixteen real screens, exported from the production Figma file", which states the wrong unit. Either "eleven screens" or "sixteen placements of eleven screens" is accurate.

2 · The log contradicts itself on screen count

§13 Deployment record says "Page weight 2.42 MB, 10 embedded screens" while §15 change history says "16 screens total" and the live page is 3.66 MB. The deployment record appears to predate both export passes.

3 · 1.25 MB of the page is duplicate base64

The three-tab index.html embeds the same screens more than once — 34.7% of the file is duplicated image data. The standalone scan/index.html carries each screen once and is 2.34 MB against the three-tab file's 3.66 MB. Deduplicating would fix it without removing anything.

4 · "0 new features built" was unsafe

Q6 says nobody was adding family members because no family overview existed — so the Family Overview was new. The stat has been changed to "0 new data or models", which is what Q1 actually supports: the intelligence already existed inside CoverRisk.
